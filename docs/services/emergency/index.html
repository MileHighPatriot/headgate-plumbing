Emergency & frozen pipes

Call any time, day or night. A person answers — not a menu — and talks you through shutting the water off while a plumber heads your way.

Signs you need us
  • Water spraying, pouring or coming through a ceiling
  • No water anywhere in the house on a cold morning
  • Sewage coming up through a floor drain
  • Water heater leaking steadily
Not sure? Try the triage tool

How we handle it

  1. Step 1

    Stop the water

    Our dispatcher walks you through finding the main shutoff and turning off the water heater while the truck is on the way.

  2. Step 2

    Make it safe

    Burst pipes repaired, backups cleared, and frozen lines thawed with safe heat — then checked for splits before the water comes back on.

  3. Step 3

    Paper trail

    Photos, a written cause, and an itemized invoice you can send straight to your insurance adjuster.

Colorado note

Most frozen pipes happen on the first hard night

Denver Water's advice: know where your shutoff is, insulate pipes on exterior walls, open sink cabinets on cold nights, drip the faucet farthest from where water enters, and keep the heat at 65°F or higher when you're away.

Straight answers

Do you charge more at night?
Yes — a flat after-hours dispatch fee, quoted on the phone before we roll. Care Plan members don't pay it.
Should I use a torch on a frozen pipe?
Never. Use a hair dryer or space heater at a safe distance, open the faucet, and work from the faucet back toward the frozen spot.
